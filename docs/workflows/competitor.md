## Procedure

Produce a deep, honest competitive teardown with a buyer-specific response. Read existing configured competitor intelligence, current product capabilities, user priorities, and any requested focus. Apply the project's voice guidance; use short direct analysis and factual positive positioning rather than puffery or unsupported attacks.

1. Start from existing intelligence, recording observation dates and confidence. Research current capabilities, pricing, and positioning through primary pages, documentation, release notes, and permitted hands-on material when access exists. Cite URLs and dates. Mark unavailable, vendor-claimed, directly observed, and inferred information distinctly. Intelligence older than six months needs a prominent stale warning; volatile claims need current verification even if newer.
2. Identify the actual buyer/category, value proposition in the competitor's words with short attributed quotations where permitted, and what the product demonstrably does. Company history/funding matters only when sourced and relevant; absence is unknown, not a made-up overview.
3. Compare capabilities at the level of meaningful buyer workflows, with strong/weak/missing/unverified ratings for both products and evidence. “Not found in public docs” does not mean absent. Explain the user consequence of each delta. Name where they win, where this product wins, and unresolved whitespace.
4. Infer likely roadmap only from observed signals, labeling forecasts and alternative explanations. Identify a structural vulnerability only when evidence supports it; otherwise describe the open hypothesis. A temporary feature gap is not necessarily an enduring weakness.
5. Recommend head-to-head message, demo proof, qualifying question, and avoid-list. Separate winning a new evaluation from displacing an installed product. Name a plausible beachhead, switching cost, wedge, trigger, and stepwise adoption path grounded in this project's buying architecture rather than another company's funnel.
6. Choose Direct threat, Worth monitoring, or Ignore for now and explain its decision implication. A direct threat calls for a roadmap/trade-off review, not reflex feature parity. Monitoring calls for a suggested revisit date/trigger; do not schedule a reminder without authorization. Identify in-flight specs whose assumptions may be invalidated.
7. Save the teardown under configured output. Propose dated sourced changes to competitor context and affected decision/spec records for review through `pb-compound`. Recommend `pb-battlecard` for live deal enablement and `pb-roadmap` for sequencing choices. Do not silently update strategic facts or contact prospects.

### Teardown depth and usable commercial output

Keep separate subsections for where they win, where we win, and where neither has won; use at least the consequential workflows needed to answer the decision, not a cosmetic feature checklist. For any “missing” rating record positive evidence of absence or its limited scope; otherwise use unverified. The likely-roadmap section names signal → inference → alternative explanation → consequence. A structural weakness must survive a plausible next feature release; distinguish that from a temporary gap. If no Achilles heel is substantiated, say so rather than manufacturing one.

Write the actual prospect talk track, not “[focus on our differentiation].” **Fictional example:** “If multi-site transfers are the priority, the rival's documented transfer flow fits that requirement. For a single-counter team, I can show today's supported reconciliation workflow and we can compare it against your end-of-day task.” The next proof question is “Which daily reconciliation step is failing now?” Avoid “We're faster and easier,” unless comparable evidence exists.

The beachhead response retains four separate answers: least locked-in relevant segment; specific wedge; adoption/displacement sequence; and observable switch trigger. Switching cost includes data, workflow, integrations, contracts and trust when evidenced. Propose a 60-day teardown revisit if no project cadence exists, with earlier re-check on material release/pricing signals; propose scheduling only. A direct-threat verdict includes the precise assumption/bet to reconsider and trade-off for the current plan, not a demand for feature parity. Proposed compounding also captures validated deal framing in stakeholder context where it belongs.

## Worked example

**Fictional example: a rental inventory product compares a fictional rival.** Supplied fixture documentation shows the rival handles multi-site transfers while the user's product provides simpler single-location stocktaking. The honest recommendation concedes the rival wins for centralized logistics, then targets independent rental counters that value fast daily reconciliation. “Our onboarding is faster” remains unsupported unless a comparable test exists. The proposed demo shows completing a real supported stocktaking flow using fictional inventory, and the displacement hypothesis is triggered by renewal complexity, not invented churn intelligence. A public-doc search failing to show offline support is recorded as unverified, not missing.

## Quality checks

Both products face the same evidentiary standard. Strengths are buyer-relevant, dated, and sourced. Forecasts and gaps remain qualified. Positioning includes concrete proof and an honest lose-case. Displacement accounts for switching friction. Verdict leads to a specific proposed decision, not automatic roadmap changes.

## Anti-patterns

Do not cheerlead, fill every competitor cell with weaknesses, equate missing docs with missing features, repeat unsupported “only” claims, confuse acquisition with displacement, invent funding/prices, or turn a threat into mandatory parity work. Do not claim monitoring has been scheduled when only recommended.
