## Procedure

Provide the outside-in landscape for a strategic decision. Read configured product, competitor, GTM/segment, and relevant constraint context. Scope the geography, market, time horizon, buyer, and decision first; an industry scan without a decision becomes a list of headlines. Use current primary market/company/regulatory sources where permitted. If research access is unavailable, label a supplied-source scan and its freshness limits rather than inventing current evidence.

1. Build an evidence ledger of facts, dates/geography, forecasts, and uncertainties. Market size, funding, adoption, regulatory, and pricing assertions require sources and defined scope. Do not present legal implications as settled without relevant jurisdiction/authority; identify needed specialist review when it affects the decision.
2. Run **SWOT** with internal strengths/weaknesses separated from external opportunities/threats. Each row explains why it matters, evidence, and the response. Do not classify a hypothetical future capability as a current strength or a trend as an internal weakness.
3. Run **PESTLE** across political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental forces. Cover all six with context-specific evidence or explicitly low relevance/insufficient data. Distinguish political direction from applicable legal requirements. Every factor should change a choice, readiness condition, or monitoring trigger; avoid generic filler.
4. Run **Porter's Five Forces**: competitive rivalry, entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. Rate intensity with supporting evidence; name substitutes including doing nothing/manual work, barriers to entry, buying/procurement leverage, and concentrated suppliers/platform dependence. Translate each into an actionable implication, not a copied theory definition.
5. Run **Ansoff** across existing/new products and existing/new markets. Describe a credible option in each quadrant: penetration, market development, product development, diversification. Choose a growth direction given evidence, stage, distribution, and capacity; compare the foregone alternative. Explicitly mark unsupported quadrants rather than manufacturing a diversification idea.
6. Integrate frameworks. Resolve apparent contradictions and avoid double-counting one trend as multiple independent facts. Show a downside/base/upside scenario only where it clarifies the decision; scenarios are assumptions, not predictions. Recommend **Do / Stop / Watch** with owner, priority, evidence, and measurable re-evaluation trigger.
7. Save under configured output. Propose sourced competitor/context updates; identify in-flight specs affected by legal/platform/market findings. Recommend `pb-competitor` for a specific emerging threat and `pb-strategy`/`pb-roadmap` for the chosen direction. Do not automatically edit plans or start monitoring agents.

### Frameworks must retain their distinct questions

SWOT keeps distinct subgroups, not a blended score: **strength** → why it matters and defensibility; **weakness** → buyer consequence and remedy; **opportunity** → timing, capture mechanism and required capacity; **threat** → likelihood basis, exposure horizon and response. Give external findings a time horizon and distinguish a observed signal from an estimated probability. A threat without a response is unfinished analysis.

In Five Forces answer the source's specific prompts: rivalry → who competes and what decides wins; entrants → entry barriers and most credible adjacent entrants; substitutes → current alternative and what would cause switching away; buyers → negotiation leverage and a reasonable concession or boundary; suppliers → actual dependence, rate/pricing/deprecation risk and fallback. A high-intensity label without actors or consequence is a theory recap. Legal findings must remain jurisdiction/source-scoped; political signals and enacted requirements are different.

Ansoff recommendation states what stays the same (product or market), what changes, evidence of reachability/value, required capability/distribution, and opportunity cost versus the strongest alternative. If a new segment requires a fundamentally new product, do not call it simple market development. In the integrated conclusion explain which evidence dominates when frameworks appear to disagree; four frameworks built on one observation provide one observation, not four confirmations. The final Do / Stop / Watch rows include owner or open assignment, timing, and a measurable revisit trigger.

**Write:** “Entering schools reuses scheduling but introduces an unverified procurement and permissions dependency; test that dependency before expansion.” **Avoid:** “Education is a huge growing market; diversify now.” The first connects external conditions to a resource-constrained choice.

## Worked example

**Fictional example: an appointment product considers entering schools.** The scan separates internal weakness in multi-site permissions from the external opportunity of centralized scheduling. Buyer power includes district procurement; substitutes include shared calendars and doing nothing. Privacy requirements are a jurisdiction-specific research gap, not a blanket invented legal ban. Ansoff identifies market development as reusing the current scheduling product for a new segment, while a new classroom-management suite would be diversification. The provisional recommendation is to validate procurement and permission requirements before expansion. “Watch” names a specific approval or pilot-demand signal rather than vague market awareness.

## Quality checks

All four frameworks are covered and produce implications. Internal/external categories are correct. Sources, time, geography, and confidence are visible. Scenarios/forecasts are distinct from observed facts. The recommended growth quadrant is logically correct and resource-aware. Unknown legal or supplier exposure is explicit.

## Anti-patterns

Do not paste generic SaaS trends, invent market-size numbers, equate regulation headlines with applicable law, call a future roadmap strength, omit doing-nothing substitutes, repeat the same fact as four independent arguments, or recommend every Ansoff quadrant simultaneously.
