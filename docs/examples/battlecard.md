# Fictional reviewer exercise: field battlecard

This is a fictional drafting example using `docs/workflows/battlecard.md`. All companies, capabilities, sources, account statements, and dates below are invented fixtures. It is not a customer artifact, a tool execution receipt, or evidence of a live host run.

Fixture brief: Lumen imports contact lists. Its supplied fictional manual says users can preview duplicate contact IDs before applying changes. Anchor's supplied fictional plan sheet includes five connected sources at a lower annual price; Lumen currently supports two. The buyer says they renewed Anchor and their account administrator prefers it. No customer outcome study, approved reference, or competitor duplicate-handling test was supplied.

## Top objections
### “We just renewed Anchor. Why would we switch?” [buyer statement A1]
Why they ask: Renewal cost is known; dissatisfaction is not established.
Response: “A switch needs enough benefit to cover the overlap and disruption. Let's test your most consequential import first and compare the effort.”
Proof: `[NEEDS DATA: representative result and migration effort]`. No referenceable outcome study supplied.

### “You only connect two of our required sources.” [anticipated; required source list unconfirmed]
Why they ask: A missing connection could make the workflow unusable.
Response: “We support two sources today. If all five are required now, Anchor is the better fit. Which sources are necessary for this import?”
Proof: P2 and C1 fixture manuals, dated 2026-09-20; account requirements need confirmation. No future connector promise.

### “How do we know this actually reduces mistakes?” [anticipated]
Why they ask: Preview visibility does not establish fewer errors.
Response: “We have not supplied an error-reduction study. We can test how your import owner handles conflicting IDs and record missed conflicts, corrections, and time.”
Proof: P1 supports preview behavior; `[NEEDS DATA: outcome result, baseline, sample, and method]`.

## Questions to ask
- “Which source connections must work before you can evaluate an import?” [Establishes fit, including a possible disqualification.]
- “How does your current workflow show two records with the same ID?” [Establishes actual incumbent behavior without asserting a weakness.]
- “Who decides which record to keep, and how do they check the result?” [Identifies workflow responsibility and validation criteria.]

## Deal strategy
Champion: Operations lead is a proposed contact, not a confirmed champion. Evaluator: account administrator is known from A1. Economic buyer: `[NEEDS DATA]`.
Incumbent champion: Administrator prefers Anchor [A1]; invite them to set the test criteria, rather than bypassing them.
Switch trigger: A recurring conflict-handling problem is a hypothesis; frequency and consequence remain unknown.
Best-fit account: A team whose necessary connections are supported and whose import owner benefits from explicit conflict review; confirm rather than assuming fit.
Migration/coexistence: No migration service or coexistence capability was supplied. Validate feasibility and full cost before offering either.
Next test: Proposed owner is the operations lead; supply one permitted representative file, run both actual workflows, and agree success criteria before concluding fit.

## What not to say
- “We cut import errors in half.” → “We can test conflict handling; we have no outcome study.”
- “Anchor can't catch duplicates.” → “We need to verify Anchor's duplicate-handling workflow.”
- “Our migration is effortless.” → “We need to estimate migration and training effort.”
- “We're like Anchor but better.” → “Our supplied manual demonstrates a review step before applying conflicting IDs.”
