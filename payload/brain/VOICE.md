# Writing rules

Apply these rules to human-readable artifacts from every Product Bench command. Read the team's reviewed `context/brand-voice.md` for product terminology, audience, examples, and approved claims. Explicit user instructions take priority. If brand context is missing, use these defaults and mark brand-specific decisions unresolved; never substitute the fictional example for team voice.

## Direct claims

Say what happens, for whom, and with what evidence. Give the positive claim directly. Avoid negative parallelism such as “This isn't X. It's Y,” “Not X, Y,” “Less X, more Y,” “Forget X,” and “They track the past; we predict the future.” A factual comparison belongs in a sourced comparison table or a precise explanation, not a slogan.

Avoid announcing importance, inventing urgency, promising transformation, or calling ordinary work unique. Replace adjectives with observable behavior. “The export includes the selected date range” beats “A seamless, powerful export experience.” Use “is” and “has” rather than “serves as,” “stands as,” “marks a,” or “boasts.” Keep uncertainty explicit rather than polishing it away.

## Words and constructions to remove

Rewrite promotional uses of: delve, realm, tapestry, paradigm, cutting-edge, revolutionize, intricate, unparalleled, pivotal, crucial, synergy, leverage, innovative, game-changer, testament, commendable, meticulous, groundbreaking, foster, holistic, pioneering, trailblazing, unleash, transformative, redefine, seamless, robust, breakthrough, empower, streamline, frictionless, elevate, effortless, insightful, visionary, disruptive, reimagine, unprecedented, intuitive, leading-edge, synergize, democratize, state-of-the-art, immersive, turnkey, future-proof, supercharge, enduring, interplay, captivate.

Treat harness, unlock, landscape, optimize, scalable, adaptive, data-driven, proactive, mission-critical, dynamic, predictive, transparent, proprietary, integrated, and plug-and-play as claims requiring concrete meaning. Replace decorative use; preserve precise technical terms, source quotations, and approved product names. Never alter a schema field or a quotation to satisfy a style rule.

Remove these filler phrases and bait:

- “In today's …,” “It's important to note,” “It's worth noting,” “In order to,” “At the end of the day,” and “Moving forward.”
- “Let's dive in,” “Let's unpack,” “In this article, I will,” and announcements of what the next section will discuss.
- “Furthermore,” “Additionally,” “Moreover,” “That said,” “With that in mind,” and “On top of that” when a direct next sentence works.
- “Let that sink in,” “Read that again,” “Full stop,” “This changes everything,” “What nobody tells you,” and unsupported “10x” claims.

## Rhythm and structure

Use active verbs, familiar words, sentence-case headings, and consistent nouns. Don't cycle through synonyms for the same actor or capability. Mix short and longer sentences; avoid a metronome of identical paragraphs. Editorial paragraphs normally have 1 or 2 sentences, with 3 when needed. Technical steps and explanations can be longer when each sentence adds information.

Use contractions in conversational scripts. Read scripts aloud: an AE should be able to say them without decoding a PM's terminology. Keep formal compliance language formal when the audience requires it. Use digits for measurements and preserve units and denominators. Use periods, commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses instead of em dashes in authored prose.

Avoid decorative triples, false “from X to Y” ranges, significance inflation, and participle endings such as “highlighting its importance.” Structural counts in a template are fine. A concrete argument earns its length. Stop when the reader has the decision or next action; omit a closing paragraph that merely repeats the document.

## Editing examples

These examples are fictional writing demonstrations, not product claims.

| Avoid | Write instead | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “It's not about dashboards. It's about decisions.” | “The weekly view links each issue to its owner and next action.” | States a behavior the reader can inspect. |
| “Our groundbreaking platform supercharges productivity.” | “The queue groups duplicate requests so the reviewer handles each issue once.” | Replaces hype with a mechanism. |
| “They can't match our enterprise security.” | “Let's verify your required access controls using the evaluation checklist.” | Uses proof instead of unverified superiority. |
| “Usage rose 40%, proving customer value.” | “Weekly active accounts rose from 10 to 14 in the fictional sample; task completion was not measured.” | Preserves the measurement's practical limit. |

## Final voice pass

Check the draft for unsupported certainty, banned filler, negative parallelism, em dashes, vague benefits, and repeated conclusions. Apply the team's approved vocabulary and review the closest audience-specific writing example. Keep quotations, code, schemas, raw observations, and factual table values faithful to their source. Style editing cannot add proof or change factual meaning.
